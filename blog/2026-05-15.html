Your Car's Modem is a Privacy Nightmare

I read a blog about someone tearing out the modem and GPS from their new RAV4. It made me rethink privacy versus convenience in modern cars.

This isn't car hacking. It's about control. DIY tech mods in vehicles are on the rise. Many feel the need to remove manufacturer-installed tracking devices.

Modern cars are computers on wheels. They have modems, GPS, and more to collect data. This data often goes back to manufacturers or third parties. It's all for 'better service' and 'enhanced user experience.'

These features offer convenience—real-time traffic updates, remote start from an app. But the trade-offs are significant.

Privacy is the main concern. We give up a lot for convenience. That feeling your car knows too much? It's valid. This tech often crosses a line.

I've seen system failures. A colleague's car software update bricked the infotainment system. The dealership needed two days to fix a 'convenience feature' issue.

Security vulnerabilities are another issue. Imagine your location data or driving habits in the wrong hands. It's a chilling thought.

People removing modems and GPS units aren't Luddites. They know that once data leaves their control, it's gone.

In tech, data misuse is common. Whether it's a careless developer or a hacker, the risk is real.

Manufacturers claim these systems are secure, but breaches happen. The cost is consumer trust.

Removing these 'features' is practical for some. They want to drive their car, not be driven by it.

It's a clash between old-school car ownership and the new model, where ownership feels like renting. The data collected rarely benefits the consumer.

Instead, it's used for services, ads, and sometimes sold. Transparency is crucial. I want to know who has my data and why.

The backlash reflects a larger trend: the desire to control our devices. It's about owning what you paid for.

There's freedom in taking something apart and rebuilding it. I get it. It's like optimizing a system or tearing down server racks.

But cars aren't servers. They're personal. They go wherever you go. For some, that justifies drastic measures.

Manufacturers need to listen. Respect privacy or face savvy customers taking matters into their own hands.

They have a chance to redefine car ownership in the tech age. Or they can watch more people reach for their toolboxes.

We shouldn't have to choose between convenience and privacy. We deserve both.
