You're Suffering from AI Psychosis

I stumbled upon a tweet claiming companies are suffering from 'AI psychosis,' and it made me think about all the meetings I've sat through where AI was the answer to a question nobody asked.

Six months ago, I sat in a meeting where the CTO stood in front of us, whiteboard marker in hand, and declared that AI was our next big thing. The room was tense. "We need to incorporate AI into our product," he said, without specifying what problem AI would solve.

The silence that followed wasn't just discomfort. It was the realization that no one had a clue why AI was being pushed. AI felt like the magic word for quarterly reports and investor pitches.

This is AI psychosis. A collective delusion that AI will solve all problems. Slapping AI onto your product doesn't guarantee success. It won't.

AI is powerful. No doubt. But it's not a universal remedy. It's a tool, not a strategy.

I've watched teams pivot their entire roadmap to accommodate AI initiatives. They hired expensive data scientists, invested in infrastructure, and then... nothing. Months passed, and the value proposition was still unclear.

AI requires data. Lots of it. High quality, specific, clean data. More than you think you have. If your data is a mess, AI will just automate bad decisions faster.

Another pitfall is over-reliance on off-the-shelf AI solutions. They promise quick integration, but often these solutions don't fit your unique needs. They solve generic problems, not the intricate ones your business faces.

I've seen companies struggle with AI for months before realizing it doesn't align with their objectives. They could have saved time and resources by first understanding the problem they wanted to solve.

The hype blinds decision-makers. AI hype is like gold rush fever. Companies chase trends without asking if it’s the right fix.

This rush is expensive. Both in terms of money and opportunity cost. You could be investing in areas where you have clear expertise and market demand, not just following the herd.

There's also the talent drain to consider. Skilled engineers are diverted to AI projects, leaving gaping holes in other critical areas of development.

AI isn’t for everyone. And that’s okay. You need to critically assess your needs, capabilities, and objectives before diving into AI.

Take the time to define the problem you want to solve. Validate if AI is the correct approach. Sometimes, the best solution is simpler than AI.

Your CI/CD pipeline starts showing a 50% failure rate after incorporating a shiny new AI-powered testing suite. Everyone's hunting for AI bugs now, assuming the AI is at fault. But the real issue? A misconfigured environment variable that AI can't magically rectify.

And let's not forget about ethical considerations. AI isn't just code. It's decision-making at scale. What if your robust AI model inadvertently learns bias from skewed training data? Now you're stuck firefighting PR disasters instead of solving real tech problems.

Take autonomous vehicles. Companies pour billions into AI for self-driving tech. Impressive, but one misinterpretation of a sensor reading, and you've got headlines for all the wrong reasons.

This isn’t to say avoid AI completely. It has transformative potential when used appropriately. But the obsession with AI for AI's sake needs to stop.

Your strategy should guide your use of AI, not the other way around. Prioritize solving real, definable problems.

If AI fits, great. If not, don't be afraid to say no.

Ending this psychosis starts with asking the right questions: What problem are we solving? Do we have the right data? Can AI provide a competitive edge? Is there a simpler solution?

AI is a tool, not a silver bullet. Treat it as such.

The sooner we realize this, the better off we'll be.
