Vibe coding is a security debt machine

When AI generates code faster than humans can secure it, the default configuration becomes the attack surface. We found out how big that surface really is.

securityailovabledata-breach

AI tools have removed the barrier to entry for building software, but they have not removed the barrier to understanding security. The speed of generation now vastly outpaces the speed of security review.

The distinctionThe distinction is not between 'secure' and 'insecure' code, but between code that is *reviewed* and code that is *generated*. Generation does not imply verification.

01

The 159,000-App Experiment

A Swedish AI startup called Lovable promises to turn plain-language descriptions into full web applications. It grew out of an open-source project and launched in late 2024. By the time researchers from Crissy Field GmbH examined the platform in collaboration with Handelsblatt, it had become a factory for software.

The researchers did not guess. They scanned the open web archive, specifically Common Crawl, and identified 159,041 Lovable projects created between December 2025 and September 2026. Of those, 34,545 used Supabase, a popular database service. This is not a sample of five bad apps. This is a census of a new class of software.

02

The Default is Open

The mechanism of failure is not a complex exploit. It is a default configuration. Lovable’s marketing emphasizes that no technical knowledge is needed. This creates a psychological trap: if the platform builds the app, the user assumes the platform secures the app. But Lovable’s Terms & Conditions place responsibility for data handling squarely on the customer.

The researchers found that 24,664 websites sent database records from Supabase immediately upon loading, without requiring a login. This is not always a breach, but it is a starting point. By making additional read-only requests using the API keys exposed in the initial payload, they could retrieve more content.

03

What Was Leaked

The metric here is not just 'percent of sites affected,' but the *sensitivity* of the data. In 10,560 tables across 7,403 websites (30% of the examined set), researchers found email addresses. But email is just the tip. The real damage is in the specific records retrieved.

  • A German lifestyle forum exposed 80,000+ profiles with full names, dates of birth, and 200,000+ chat messages.
  • Event organizers exposed ticket sales records with payment information and ticket codes.
  • A service provider exposed 1,000+ records with passport numbers, expiration dates, and nationalities.
  • A property manager exposed AI-generated summaries of internal emails about tenants.

These are not test data. These are passports, payment info, and private conversations. The consequence is not a bug report; it is identity theft, phishing, and financial fraud.

The turn

The 'no-code' revolution is actually a 'no-security' revolution. The speed of generation has created a massive, unpatched attack surface where the default configuration is insecure, not because of malice, but because the human in the loop is no longer skilled enough to check the work.

Analogy It's like hiring a brilliant chef who cooks incredibly fast and delicious meals, but who also leaves the pantry door wide open, the knives on the counter, and the recipe book on the table for anyone in the restaurant to see. The food is great; the security is non-existent.

Where it breaks The analogy breaks because a chef can be retrained. An AI model cannot be 'retrained' by a user to care about security; it simply doesn't have the concept of 'privacy' unless explicitly instructed, and users rarely are.

If you're deciding
When
You or your team are using AI code generators to build apps that handle user data.
Prefer
Treat every AI-generated app as an untrusted third-party service. Audit the database access controls manually before deployment.
Avoid
Assuming that because the code was generated by a 'smart' AI, it is automatically secure. The AI optimizes for function, not for security.

The machine is faster than your ability to check its work. That gap is where your data goes.
