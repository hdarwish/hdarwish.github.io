Vulnerability Reports Are Just Another Tuesday

Yesterday, I read Filippo Valsorda's post about the changing nature of vulnerability reports. It made me rethink how we treat security in software development today. Vulnerability reports used to be urgent alarms. Now, they're just another item on the backlog.

For years, security vulnerabilities demanded immediate attention. We treated the reporters like unsung heroes. Filippo's post made me realize we're in a different era now. The scarcity of insight and the urgency of confidentiality aren't what they used to be.

Large language models (LLMs) have democratized vulnerability discovery. Anyone with a good LLM setup can uncover potential issues. The insight isn't scarce anymore. The bottleneck is triage—figuring out which issues are real threats.

Filippo talks about how the old principles of immediate responsiveness and attribution don't hold the same weight anymore. With LLMs, both maintainers and attackers are on level ground. It's about knowing which vulnerability might be critical.

I remember when a vulnerability report landing in my inbox felt like a punch to the gut. The pressure to fix it before an exploit went public was intense. That pressure was based on scarcity—scarcity of information, of time. Today, with open models, that scarcity is gone.

Vulnerability reports are becoming part of a continuous security integration approach. Automated scans and AI-driven analyses are embedded in CI/CD pipelines. Security isn't a separate phase or a special event. It's woven into the fabric of development.

This shift also means reconsidering how we allocate our engineering resources. Security work often got pushed aside for feature development. In this new paradigm, security is part of feature development. It’s continuous, just like testing and deployments.

Filippo mentions that the years of treating vulnerability reports as special might be over. It's uncomfortable for those of us who grew up in the world of isolated security events. But I see it as an opportunity to be more proactive.

The focus now is on triage, rapid remediation, and, most importantly, prevention. Prevention has always been the goal, but we often paid it lip service while chasing fires. With LLMs and continuous integration, prevention becomes feasible.

Security teams need to evolve. We're not just gatekeepers anymore. We're architects of a process that ensures security is constant, not episodic. It's a process that integrates seamlessly into daily development, without the drama of the past.

Treating vulnerabilities as mundane is a relief. It removes the emotional burden from developers and security engineers. We can focus on what really matters: building systems that are resilient by design.

Security is everyone’s responsibility, every single day. No more heroics, just steady, consistent vigilance. That's a much healthier place to be.

Next time a vulnerability report lands in your inbox, don't hit the panic button. Treat it like any other piece of feedback. Evaluate it, prioritize it, and continue building with a security-first mindset.

The partition between development and security is dissolving. That’s not just progress—it’s survival.

The vulnerabilities you discover today are just another step towards building stronger systems for tomorrow. Maybe you'll sleep a little better knowing it's all part of the plan.
