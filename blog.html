Why We're Overthinking Concurrency and Underestimating Creativity with AI

Let's be honest: the way we handle concurrency has always been a bit convoluted. Traditional multi-threading in languages like Java feels like assembling a Rube Goldberg machine for a task that could be handled with a hammer. Enter Go, with its goroutines and channels, offering a more fluid, intuitive approach to concurrency. You might think it's just syntactic sugar over threads, but it's more than that. Go abstracts away the complexity, and that's not a trivial benefit—it fundamentally shifts how we design concurrent applications.

In Go, goroutines are cheap, and channels make communication between them clear and safe. This reduces the need to micromanage threads and locks, letting us focus on the real task at hand. What I find interesting is not just the performance boost from Go's model, but the mental space it frees up for developers. Less time worrying about deadlocks means more cognitive bandwidth for solving actual problems. It’s like clearing out your garage so you can finally work on that project car.

But what if we take this idea beyond programming frameworks and look at problem-solving itself? Recently, an amateur mathematician armed with ChatGPT reportedly solved a decades-old Erdős problem. This isn’t just a quirky story about AI—it's a profound example of how AI can augment human creativity, breaking down traditional boundaries of expertise. AI, especially models like GPT, acts almost like a concurrency model for human thought, handling some cognitive loads so we can focus on higher-level creativity.

There's a parallel to be drawn here with model parallelism in PyTorch. When tackling massive deep learning models, distributing computations across multiple devices is crucial. But the challenge is managing this distribution without introducing inefficiencies. PyTorch offers tools for model parallelism that, like Go's goroutines, aim to make a complex task more digestible. It’s about reducing overhead—not just computationally, but also cognitively for us, the humans writing the code.

So, what does this all mean for us as engineers? It's a reminder to not settle for the status quo in problem-solving. Whether it's rethinking concurrency models or leveraging AI for creative breakthroughs, there's an untapped potential in questioning traditional methods. And while Go and PyTorch push boundaries in computational efficiency, AI like ChatGPT pushes the boundaries of what we can achieve creatively.

Here's what I'm genuinely curious about: As AI continues to evolve, will it reshape not only how we solve problems but how we think about them from the ground up? Will our mental models for coding and creativity adapt to a point where these tools not only assist but also inspire us to seek solutions we hadn't imagined?
